## 🧠 1.5.1 Internet Access Technologies

Different internet access technologies are available depending on **geographical location, cost, availability, and network requirements.**

Homes and businesses connect to the internet through an **Internet Service Provider (ISP)**.

An **ISP** is a company that provides customers with access to the internet.

**Example:** A household using a broadband service from **BT** or **Virgin Media** to access the internet.

## 🧠 1.5.2 Home and Small Office Internet Connections
Homes and SOHO networks commonly use the following internet connection technologies:

### Cable Broadband

**Cable broadband** provides internet access using the same cable network that delivers cable television.
    > Different services carried over the same cable infrastructure.

- Common in residential areas.
- Provides high-speed internet access.
- Bandwidth is often shared with other customers in the same area.

Example: A household receives television and internet services through the same cable provider.

### DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)

**DSL** provides internet access using existing copper telephone lines.

- Provides high availability, and an always-on connection to the internet.
- Performance depends on the DSL technology and line conditions.
- Speeds may decrease over longer copper distances.

NOTE: 
Home offices and small businesses may use **ADSL (Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line)**, which provides higher download speeds than upload speeds.
    > ADSL allocates more capacity to downloading because many home users receive more data than they send.

Example: A home office using a telephone line to access the internet through a DSL modem.

> NOTE: DSL vs Dial-up
>
> DSL: Provides an always-on internet connection while allowing telephone calls.
> Dial-up: An older internet connection technology that uses a telephone line and a dial-up modem. Provides very low bandwidth and typically prevents telephone calls while connected.

### Cellular

**Cellular internet access** uses a cell phone network to connect, such as 4G or 5G.
    > If you can get a cellular signal and an active mobile data service, you can get cellular internet access.

- Connects through a mobile network provider.
- Useful when fixed broadband is unavailable.
- Performance depends on signal strength, the device, and network congestion.

Example: Using a smartphone's mobile hotspot to connect a laptop to the internet.
    > The laptop uses Wi-Fi to connect to the phone, while the phone uses cellular data to access the internet.

### Satellite

**Satellite internet** provides internet access through communication satellites, especially in areas where other connection methods are unavailable.

- Useful in rural or remote areas.
- Requires suitable satellite equipment.
- Performance depends on the satellite system being used.

Example: A family living in a remote countryside location uses satellite internet to access websites, send emails, and make video calls.

### Fibre Broadband

**Fibre Broadband** uses fibre-optic cables to provide high-speed internet access.

- Provides high bandwidth and can support symmetrical upload and download speeds.
- Resistant to electromagnetic interference.
- Availability depends on local infrastructure.

Example: A household uses fibre broadband from BT to connect multiple devices to the internet for streaming, online gaming, and working from home.

## 🧠 1.5.3 Businesses Internet Connections

Businesses often require more reliable internet connectivity, higher bandwidth, and support for many users and services.

Common connection options include:

### Dedicated Leased Line

A dedicated connection provided by a telecommunications company.

- Provides a business with dedicated bandwidth rather than sharing that access capacity with neighbouring customers.
- Often offers symmetrical bandwidth (same upload and download speeds).
- The provider typically guarantees certain service standards through a contract.

Example: A company uses a dedicated leased line from BT to provide reliable internet connectivity for its employees to upload large files, attend video conferences, and access cloud services.

### Metro Ethernet (Ethernet WAN)

**Metro Ethernet** is a service that uses Ethernet technology to connect business locations across a city or metropolitan area.
    > Metro Ethernets extend LAN access technology into the WAN.

- Can connect multiple business sites.
- Commonly offered by telecommunications providers.

Example: 
A company has three offices:
- Office A: South London
- Office B: East London
- Office C: Central London 

> Each office has its own LAN, connecting computers, printers, and other devices.
> The company uses a telecommunications provider, such as BT, to connect these LANs through a **Metro Ethernet service**.
> Now, an employee in Office A can access a file server located in Office B.
    
### Business DSL

A DSL connection used by businesses where suitable infrastructure is available.

- Uses telephone lines.
- May be less expensive than dedicated connectivity.
- Performance depends on the available service.

> NOTE:
>
>They might use Symmetric Digital Subscriber Line (SDSL) which is similar to the consumer version of DSL but provides uploads and downloads at the same high speeds. 

### Satellite

An option for businesses operating in remote locations without suitable fixed-line infrastructure.

Example: A company connecting its offices in London and Manchester through a telecommunications provider.

### Fiber Internet 

**Fibre Broadband** uses fibre-optic cables to provide high-speed internet access with symmetrical upload and download speed. 

## 🧠 1.5.4 The Converging Network

Traditionally, organisations used separate networks for different communication services:

**Data Networks:** Computers, file sharing, and internet access.
**Telephone Networks:** Voice communication.
**Video Networks:** Video conferencing and other video services.

Each network used different technologies to carry the communication signal and it had its own set of rules and standards to ensure successful communication.

Today, a **converged network** combines these services into a single network infrastructure.

Real-World Example

A university uses one IP-based network to support:

- Computers accessing websites and learning platforms.
- VoIP (Voice over IP) telephones.
- Video conferencing.
- File sharing and other network applications.

Instead of maintaining completely separate networks for each service, they can share the same infrastructure.

## 📌 Key Concepts to Remember

- An ISP (Internet Service Provider) provides internet connectivity.
- Home and SOHO networks may use cable, DSL, cellular, satellite, or fibre broadband.
- Internet access technologies differ in bandwidth, cost, availability, and reliability.
- Businesses may use dedicated leased lines, Metro Ethernet, or other provider services.
- A converged network carries data, voice, and video over shared network infrastructure.
- Network connection choices depend on the needs of users and organisations.


## 🔐 Security Relevance

- Understanding internet connections helps cybersecurity professionals identify how networks connect to external services and where security controls are needed.
- Wireless and Cellular Security: Secure wireless access and protect connected devices.
- Remote Connectivity: Use encrypted VPNs or other secure access methods when appropriate.
- Business Connectivity: Protect communication between company locations.
- Converged Network Security: Apply appropriate segmentation and security controls to protect voice, video, and data services sharing the same infrastructure.
