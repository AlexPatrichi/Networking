## 🎯 Objective 
- Understand how networks differ in size and geographical coverage.

- Explain how the internet connects networks worldwide.
- Understand the differences between intranets and extranets.

## 🧠 1.4.1 — Networks of Many Sizes

Networks can vary in size, from small home networks to large networks connecting millions of devices.

The internet connect networks worldwide and is the largest internetwork in existence.

### Examples of Different Network Sizes

- **Small Home Networks:** Connect personal devices such as laptops, smartphones, and printers.
- **SOHO (Small Office / Home Office):** A small network used by home offices or small businesses to share resources and access the internet.
- **Medium to Large Networks:** Connect departments, buildings, and potentially multiple locations.
- **Worldwide Networks:** Connects networks across countries and continents.

## 🧠 1.4.2 LANs and WANs

Two common types of network infrastructure are **Local Area Networks (LANs)** and **Wide Area Networks (WANs).**

### Local Area Network (LAN)

A **LAN** connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, office, or university building.

- Covers a relatively small area.
- Usually managed by a single individual or organisation.
- Typically provides high-speed connectivity between local devices.

**Example:** Computers connected to the same network switch in a university computer lab.

### Wide Area Network (WAN)

A **WAN** connects networks across larger geographical distances, such as different cities or countries.

- Covers a large geographical area.
- Connects multiple LANs.
- Often uses telecommunications provider infrastructure (ISPs).

**Example:** A university connecting its campus network to another campus in a different city.

## 🧠 1.4.3 The Internet

The **internet** is a worldwide **network of networks**, connecting private and public networks across the globe.

It connects homes, businesses, universities, governments, and other organisations.

The Internet is not **owned or controlled** by any individual or group. Instead, multiple organisations help **develop standards, coordinate resources, and maintain its operation.**

**Example:** A student accessing an online learning platform hosted on a server in another country.

## 🧠 1.4.4 Intranets and Extranets

### Intranet

An intranet is a private connection of LANs and WANs that belongs to an organisation.
It provides authorised users with access to internal information and resources.

**Example:** A hospital's internal staff accessing internal hospital resources.

### Extranet

An extranet provides authorised external users with controlled access to selected organisational resources.

**Example:** External GP practices accessing selected hospital resources with permission.

⚠️  It qualifies as an extranet when external users receive controlled access to the private resources.

## 📌 Key Concepts to Remember

- Networks vary in size, from small home networks to large worldwide networks.
- SOHO (Small Office / Home Office) networks allow small businesses and home offices to share resources and access the internet.
- LAN (Local Area Network): Connects devices within a limited geographical area.
- WAN (Wide Area Network): Connects networks across larger geographical distances.
- Internet: The world's largest internetwork, connecting public and private networks globally.
- Intranet: A private organisational network accessible to authorised users.
- Extranet: Provides authorised external users with controlled access to selected organisational resources.

## 🔐 Security Relevance

Understanding different network types helps cybersecurity professionals protect network infrastructure and control access to resources.

- **LAN Security:** Protect local devices and resources from unauthorised access.
- **WAN Security:** Secure communication between geographically separated networks.
- **Internet Security:** Use firewalls, encryption, and secure protocols to protect network traffic.
- **Intranet Security:** Restrict access to internal organisational resources.
- **Extranet Security:** Ensure external users can access only the resources they are authorised to use.
